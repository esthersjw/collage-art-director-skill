# Theme-specific visual language

Use this document to avoid generic collage decoration. Select one primary family and, at most, one supporting family.

## Pets

Good material cues:

- hand-drawn whisker lines, paw-scale marks, yarn, pet-tag shapes;
- photocopied fur texture, soft fabric patches, toy-like flat forms;
- humorous microcopy that follows posture or gaze.

Avoid:

- random hearts and stars;
- adding collars, toys, food, or expressions not present in the source;
- turning the pet into a glossy 3D mascot unless explicitly requested.

## Food and fast food

Good material cues:

- menu typography, order tickets, greaseproof paper, tray liners;
- condiment-color blocks used abstractly;
- receipt numbers, wrappers, check patterns, hand-drawn ingredient marks.

Avoid:

- fake sauce splashes, floating ingredients, steam, blur, or glossy advertising effects;
- inventing food that competes with the photographed subject;
- generic café stickers unrelated to the specific food.

## Travel

Good material cues:

- ticket fragments, map lines, date stamps, transit colors, luggage tags;
- location names as microcopy;
- photographed details cropped as souvenirs.

Avoid:

- fake passport stamps or airline logos;
- universal airplane and globe stickers when the image has stronger local cues.

## Events and friendship

Good material cues:

- date, venue, small quotations, snapshot frames, contact-sheet fragments;
- handwriting used sparingly;
- one recurring color sampled from clothes or environment.

Avoid:

- equal photo grids;
- “best day ever” filler text;
- excessive tape on every image.

## Fashion and portrait

Good material cues:

- editorial type, garment details, seams, fabric patches, silhouette echoes;
- large words behind and in front of the subject;
- restrained credits or issue-number microcopy.

Avoid:

- beauty-retouch blur;
- decorative elements covering eyes, hands, or key clothing details;
- forced luxury gradients and fake magazine logos.

## Speaking, performance, and work

Good material cues:

- transcript fragments, waveform-like marker lines, note margins;
- one oversized verb, compact supporting statement;
- microphone, gesture, or presentation material treated as structural anchors.

Avoid:

- corporate slide templates;
- speech bubbles unless the concept is explicitly comic;
- placing all copy in an unused corner.

## Nature and calm daily life

Good material cues:

- quiet paper fields, botanical silhouettes derived from the actual scene;
- restrained handwritten annotation;
- one large breathing area with controlled type.

Avoid:

- dreamy blur and fog;
- unrelated flowers, moons, and sparkles;
- sentimental filler copy.

## Source-derived palette

Build a palette from the uploaded photo:

- one dominant environmental color;
- one subject color;
- one dark anchor;
- one light paper color;
- optionally one contrast accent.

Check foreground–background contrast at the exact overlap area. Do not choose a title color solely because it appears in the source if it merges with the subject.

When a sampled color lacks contrast:

- shift its lightness while preserving hue character;
- use the source color for the background and a contrasting anchor for type;
- add a flat outline or offset print shadow;
- relocate the copy.
